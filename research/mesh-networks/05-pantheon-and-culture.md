## Summary

The piece draws cultural energy from fiction and movement history but lifts
nothing. **Pantheon** (AMC+ 2022, Amazon Prime 2023; created by Craig
Silverstein, based on Ken Liu's short stories; two seasons, sixteen episodes)
spends its second season on exactly our thought experiment: after uploaded
intelligences crash the global internet, Maddie and friends bootstrap a
homemade intranet called "Lilypad." **Cory Doctorow's *Walkaway*** (2017, Tor
Books) imagines resilient, bottom-up infrastructure as a political project. The
**cypherpunk lineage** — Tim May, Eric Hughes, John Gilmore, and the 1992
mailing list — supplies the older idea that cryptography, not authority, can
underwrite a network. And the 2025–26 **cyberpunk revival** is the present-day
mood: patched-together, post-everything tech, described here in our own words,
never scraped.

## Findings

### 1. Pantheon (C-40) — inspiration, with a hard line

*Pantheon* is an adult animated SF drama created by Craig Silverstein, adapting
Ken Liu's short stories; Season 1 ran on AMC+ in 2022, Season 2 on Amazon Prime
in 2023 (sixteen episodes total). Its Season 2 opening is uncanny for this
article: with the world's internet and power knocked out, the characters build a
small local network — "Lilypad" — to keep talking. We credit the show as the
most serious recent screen treatment of post-cloud, decentralised infrastructure.
**Discipline:** name it as inspiration only. No dialogue, screenshots, frame
grabs, character designs, or plot text appear on the page — the article describes
*that the show exists and what theme it shares*, nothing more.

### 2. Walkaway and the cypherpunks (C-41, C-42)

Doctorow's *Walkaway* (2017) is the political imagination of resilient,
collectively-built infrastructure — relevant to § III's "what would we build"
framing. Behind both fiction and practice sits the cypherpunk lineage: Tim May,
Eric Hughes, and John Gilmore, the mailing list founded in 1992, and Hughes'
1993 manifesto — the durable claim that privacy and trust can be built from
cryptography rather than granted by an authority. This is the intellectual root
of the web-of-trust and Reticulum ideas in chunk 03.

### 3. The 2025–26 cyberpunk revival (observed)

The current revival — visible across TikTok edits and Are.na channels — trades
the chrome-and-neon of 1980s cyberpunk for something grubbier: salvaged
hardware, mesh radios, "what does a post-everything stack look like." It's a mood,
not a source. The article describes the vibe in original prose and embeds or
scrapes nothing.
