## Summary

The plan asks the piece to "separate mechanism from ritual, without dismissing
either." These are the myths worth addressing, each with the mechanism that
corrects it and a source.

## Myths

### 1. "More crema means better coffee." → folklore

Crema tracks **freshness and CO₂ load**, not flavor quality. Stale beans foam
less; robusta foams more than arabica despite lower regard. Crema is a real,
structured foam (chunk 07) and a freshness cue — but not a taste verdict.
*Source: Illy & Navarini 2011.*

### 2. "Tamp as hard as you can." → mostly ritual

Within the tested range, tamp force showed no appreciable effect on shot time or
extraction yield; the authors standardized at 98 N. The tamp's real job is to
*level* the bed so water enters evenly. *Source: Cameron & Hendon 2020.*

### 3. "Finer grind always extracts more." → false past a point

Under homogeneous flow the model says yes — but real beds channel. EY peaks then
*falls* as grind gets finer, because fine particles clog and water carves
preferential paths. *Source: Cameron & Hendon 2020 (Fig. 4).*

### 4. "Chase the 25-second shot clock." → outdated as a control variable

The paper argues time should be treated as an *outcome*, not a dial: navigate the
yield landscape with coffee mass and water mass (brew ratio), and shots may even
finish in <15 s. The 25 s figure is a historical SCA descriptor, not a control
target. *Source: Cameron & Hendon 2020.*

### 5. "9 bar is the one true pressure." → it's a standard, not a law

9 bar is the historical/sensory standard (and what an OPV caps to). The *Matter*
model finds yield actually rises at lower pressure, and their café trials ran at
6 bar to avoid clogging. Modern machines deliberately *profile* pressure over the
shot. So 9 bar is a convention worth understanding, not a constant the machine
must hold flat. *Sources: SCA via Cameron & Hendon 2020; chunk 06.*

### 6. "Espresso is just strong/over-roasted coffee." → it's a method

Espresso is defined by *process* — pressure-driven percolation through a fine bed
— not by roast or bean. The same beans brewed as filter aren't espresso.
*Source: Cameron & Hendon 2020 (definition).*
